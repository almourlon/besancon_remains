BMB Ms 1054 · Besançon Municipal Library

The Manuscript

A small volume compiled in 1715 preserves more than a century of Bisontin voices. Its material form, the order of its texts and the hand that copied them are all part of the history reconstructed by this edition.

The manuscript is held by the Bibliothèque municipale de Besançon and is available online through its Mémoire vive digital library. Its title describes the volume as a collection made in Besançon in 1715:

Plusieurs dialogues, tant en françois qu’en patois, sur Besançon, receullis audit lieu l’an de grâce 1715

Its modest format contrasts with the variety of the collection. Alongside winegrowers, magistrates and a king, several monuments of Besançon are personified and allowed to speak. Latin sententiae written in the margins of the final two dialogues introduce a more learned register beside the French and local patois of the main texts.

II

Several histories in one volume

The Compilation

The date 1715 belongs to the surviving collection, not necessarily to every work it contains. The nine pieces are not arranged according to the chronology of the events they represent. Their subjects extend from Henri IV’s campaign against Besançon in 1595 to the celebrations of the peace of 1714, and their present order may simply reflect the sequence in which exemplars reached the compiler.

  1. 1595–1714 Events represented The dates staged or named by the texts.
  2. 1668 / 1695 Earlier witnesses Evidence that some pieces circulated independently.
  3. 1715 Surviving compilation The moment when the nine pieces were copied together in Ms 1054.

Printed witness P

1668

The Pillory and the Porte Noire

An independent printed version proves that this dialogue circulated almost half a century before Ms 1054. Its readings differ from those of the manuscript, including in some explicitly anti-French passages.

Manuscript witness Z

1695

The final two dialogues

Ms Z 428 preserves earlier versions of the final dialogues. Variants range from individual words and lines to stage directions found in Z but absent from the 1715 copy.

Ms 1054 is therefore a point of convergence rather than the original context of every text. The surviving witnesses establish several distinct textual histories, but they do not reveal every intermediate stage. The authors of the nine pieces remain anonymous.

III

Attributing the copy

Hands and Transmission

An ex libris in Ms 1054 names Pierre-François Barberot, a lawyer at the Parlement and a citizen of Besançon. Ownership alone does not identify a copyist. A second manuscript, however, provides a basis for comparison: BMB Ms 1007 contains writing associated with Barberot, and its general appearance and individual letter forms closely resemble the hand of Ms 1054.

Exploratory digital comparison

Testing the palaeographical impression

The experiment compares samples from Ms 1007 and Ms 1054. It was designed to support visual examination, not to replace it or to function as an automatic identification of the scribe.

  1. 01

    Comparable contexts

    Seven words occurring in both manuscripts were selected so that cursive letters could be studied in similar contexts.

  2. 02

    A small corpus

    Sixty-four characters were extracted from each manuscript: 128 letter images in total.

  3. 03

    Image preparation

    The characters were binarised, resized, centred and converted into pixel vectors.

  4. 04

    Similarity measurement

    Cosine similarity was calculated between the corresponding forms. Many scores exceeded 0.75; some approached or surpassed 0.90.

What the experiment cannot prove

The sample is small, pixel comparison is sensitive to scale and position, and one writer’s forms naturally vary. The results are therefore corroborative, not demonstrative: they support the visual comparison but cannot establish scribal identity on their own.

Degrees of certainty

What the evidence supports

  1. Attested Barberot owned Ms 1054. His ex libris appears in the volume.
  2. Strongly supported Barberot copied Ms 1054. Visual and exploratory digital comparisons with Ms 1007 point to the same hand.
  3. Plausible Barberot assembled the surviving collection. The consistent hand, ownership and title make this likely, but do not prove how the pieces were selected or ordered.
  4. Not established Barberot authored or revised the texts. The individual authors remain unknown, and the surviving variants may derive from lost exemplars.
